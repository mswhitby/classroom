## Day 5: Weekly practice check and corrections



### Weekly check



**Q1: B.** 0.15 × 80 = 12.



Distractors: A: Finds only 10%. C: Uses the percent number as the amount. D: Subtracts the percent amount instead of reporting it.



**Q2: C.** The savings are $12, leaving 48 − 12 = $36.



Distractors: A: Reports savings, not sale price. B: Subtracts 25 dollars rather than 25%. D: Adds the savings instead of subtracting.



**Q3: C.** The $10 increase is 10/50 = 20% of the original price.



Distractors: A: Confuses dollar change with percent change. B: Divides by the new price. D: Reports the new price as a percent of the original, not the increase.



**Q4: B.** Add 7, then divide by 4: x = 7.



Distractors: A: Subtracts 7 instead of adding it. C: Does not consistently isolate x. D: Stops before dividing by 4.



**Q5: A.** Divide by 3: x + 2 = 8. Subtract 2: x = 6.



Distractors: B: Stops at x + 2 = 8. C: Adds 2 instead of subtracting. D: Subtracts 2 before accounting for the factor of 3.



**Q6: B.** (19 − 7)/(6 − 2) = 12/4 = 3.



Distractors: A: Reverses rise and run. C: Uses only the change in y. D: Adds the y-values.



**Q7: A.** The rate is 6/2 = 3 and y = 5 at x = 0, so y = 3x + 5.



Distractors: B: Swaps the rate and initial value. C: Uses the y step without dividing by the x step. D: Omits the initial value.



**Q8: C.** C = 2(4) + 9 = $17.



Distractors: A: Omits the starting charge. B: Adds the rate to the starting charge without multiplying by hours. D: Multiplies both the rate and starting charge by hours.



**Q9: B.** 14 + 2h = 4 + 4h gives 10 = 2h, so h = 5. Both cost $24.



Distractors: A: Uses only the difference between hourly rates. C: Uses the sum of starting fees divided by the rate difference. D: Stops at 10 = 2h.



**Q10: A.** A costs $20; B costs $22. Plan A is cheaper by $2.



Distractors: B: May compare only the starting fees. C: May assume all comparisons are at the crossover. D: The supplied input and both models are enough to compare.
