# Give a team page its structure

## Start with a page someone can use

Your school’s robotics team needs a page for its next meetup. Before picking colors or animations, decide what visitors need to know: **who**, **when**, and **where**.

**HTML** describes a page’s structure and meaning. A **heading** introduces a topic. A **paragraph** holds related sentences. A **link** takes someone somewhere else.

```html
<h1>Robotics Club</h1>
<p>Build something together this Thursday.</p>
```

An **element** usually has an opening tag, content, and a closing tag. In `<h1>Robotics Club</h1>`, the heading’s content is “Robotics Club.” Tags describe that content; they aren’t words the visitor sees.

## Make the next step clear

```html
<a href="https://www.firstinspires.org/">Explore robotics</a>
```

The `href` **attribute** gives the link its destination. Use words that describe where the link goes. “Explore robotics” tells a visitor more than “click here.”

## Your turn

Turn the starter into a meetup announcement. Add a main heading, a paragraph that includes a meeting time, and a useful link. Run it to see your page. Then check your work.

Try changing the heading to `<p>`. The words still appear, but what meaning did you lose? A page’s structure helps people using screen readers navigate, too.

## Stop and think

- Which information deserves the main heading?
- How is an HTML element different from its text?
- Can someone understand your link without reading the sentence around it?
